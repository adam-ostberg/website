AI can be an incredibly useful tool when it comes to studying, at least if approached correctly.
The main thing to remember is that we are at university to learn, and the aim with using AI should be exactly that. I try not to use AI just to make it easier to pass a course.
Personally, I use and pay for Claude, but everything discussed in this post will work with any of the major LLMs. I have split this article into different sections depending on the kind of schoolwork we are talking about.

## A general tip before we start

If I had to give one single biggest-impact tip before we dive deeper it would be to use your AI tools "projects" feature. Most of the big ones have one and they are really useful for the purpose of studying. What I do is that I create a new project for every course I am currently studying. There I upload presentation slides and relevant PDFs to give the LLM as much context as possible of the course and its material. You can also give it course specific instructions, for example I often tell it to only refer to actual course material (if relevant) when answering questions. This avoids the AI referencing material not relevant to your course or using other standards for variable names etc.

## Exercise assignments

The main goal with these assignments is not the end product, unlike for example projects, they are here to improve our problem solving and ability to apply concepts.
For me, using AI to solve these kinds of problems defeats their whole purpose and I really try not to get into the habit of it. Sometimes it's definitely tempting to give in, especially if you have
been stuck on a certain problem for a long time, but I try to do the following instead:

1. I use the AI to test my knowledge of the underlying concepts and deepen them.
2. I get the AI to *generate different easier problems* to practice applying these concepts to real problems. This is actually a really good way to build up the ability to solve a harder problem yourself.
3. Make the AI give me hints about the problem. **Note:** I have some comments about this below.

{% note %}

Be careful when asking AI for hints. There are two main traps here. Firstly, is it even allowed? If it's a graded assignment then most courses prohibit the use of AI to solve the problem, and this is then kind of a grey zone. Secondly, it often leads to asking for more hints and (consciously or not) more or less leads to having the AI indirectly solve the problem for you.

{% endnote %}

## Course Projects

Projects is where I think LLMs can really shine in your studies. I believe any engineer needs to get used to working with AI tools, and the best way to do this is to use them in real projects. I also find that it allows you to increase the scope and ambition levels of your projects a whole lot, without becoming overwhelming.

{% note %}

From my experience most courses at my university allow us to use LLMs in projects as long as we disclose how we use it, and still understand every part of the project.

{% endnote %}

### My usual project setup

I personally use *Claude Code* in the terminal when working on projects. I recommend setting up a `CLAUDE.md` file, or similar, to give your LLM not only context about the course, but also the specific project/assignment.

I think the challenge for projects, at least for me, is to manage the scale of it. Current AI models are capable of creating incredible programs at incredible scale and speed. Nothing is stopping you from going ham with it, which you sometimes should. It really gives you a new perspective on what you can accomplish with these tools. Though with school projects I always try to make sure I understand each piece of the software that is being developed. Initially, this means "holding back" a little bit and progress being slower (still much faster than pre-AI). In the end I find that I don't only learn more this way, but also that the software ends up more focused and less "bloaty".

## Exam studying

Nowadays, my exam preparation consists mostly of chatting with AI to check and deepen my understanding of concepts. Then, when I feel ready I begin practising on older exams. I find AI can help in two main ways here.

1. For courses that don't provide many past exams (some only give you one), you can give the LLM all the available exams and then ask it to generate a new unique (but similar) exam.
2. Use it to help you target your weak areas.
    - Give it your answers/results and make it analyze which areas you already know well enough, and which you should focus your time on.
    - For your weak areas, you can make it generate quizzes targeting just those concepts until you know them by heart.

Used this way, AI doesn't replace the studying, it instead makes the time you spend on it more focused. Whatever the kind of schoolwork, the question I keep coming back to is whether I'm using it to learn the material or just to get past it.
